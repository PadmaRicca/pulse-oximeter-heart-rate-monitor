# Pulse Oximeter and Heart Rate Monitoring System

**Project Level:** Advanced Undergraduate

**Institution:** Middlesex University London  
**Degree:** BEng (Hons) Biomedical Engineering  
**Module:** BMS3636 (Principles of Medical Electronics)  
**Academic Year:** 3rd (2024/2025)  
**Assessment Outcome:** First Class (Distinction)  
**Overall Module Mark:** 2 (First Class, Distinction)  
**Project Type:** Electronic circuit design, optical biosensor prototyping, physiological signal analysis and wearable medical device concept project

## Project Overview

Designed and evaluated an Arduino-based oxygen saturation (SpO2) and heart rate monitoring system intended for patients requiring home mechanical ventilation, combining experimental prototyping with the conceptual development of a wearable medical device.

The project involved investigating reflective photoplethysmography (PPG), assessing sensor performance against a commercial pulse oximeter and developing an extended monitoring architecture incorporating additional physiological sensing and patient alert mechanisms. Experimental findings highlighted substantial measurement inaccuracies, informing recommendations for improving reliability and potential clinical applicability.

## Contributions

- Investigated the principles of pulse oximetry, physiological signal characteristics and sources of measurement error, considering the specific monitoring requirements of patients dependent on home mechanical ventilation.
- Designed an electronic circuit schematic using CirkitDesigner, integrating the MAX30102 reflective-mode biosensor, Arduino UNO and OLED display.
- Examined MAXREFDES117 reference firmware and developed technical flowcharts using draw.io to illustrate signal acquisition, filtering, physiological parameter calculations and monitoring logic.
- Assembled a physical breadboard prototype and conducted five-minute data acquisition procedures, recording red and infrared PPG signals, SpO2 and heart rate measurements through serial communication using PuTTY.
- Collected reference measurements using a HYLOGY commercial pulse oximeter and performed comparative analysis in MATLAB, producing graphical visualisations and descriptive statistics to assess sensor accuracy and reliability.
- Identified substantial discrepancies in heart rate measurements (46.06 bpm against 80.59 bpm from the commercial oximeter) and variability in SpO2 readings, evaluating limitations associated with optical interference, calibration and signal quality.
- Developed a conceptual forehead-worn monitoring system incorporating proposed temperature sensing, wireless communication, data storage and alert mechanisms, considering patient needs and relevant medical device standards.

## Skills Demonstrated
